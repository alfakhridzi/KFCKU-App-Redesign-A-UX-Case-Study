# KFCKU-App-Redesign-A-UX-Case-Study
A UX redesign of KFCKU, the food ordering app of KFC Indonesia. Through user interviews, the team found that people struggle to find promos, vouchers, and rewards, and that the home screen and checkout feel crowded. The redesign makes navigation clearer, promos more visible, menu customization easier, and checkout simpler.

Background

KFCKU was launched in 2017 by PT Fast Food Indonesia Tbk so customers can order KFC menus online without visiting a restaurant. The app already uses several UI patterns, including a Z-pattern layout, cards, sticky navigation, and search autocomplete. Even so, users still run into problems with navigation and with how features are arranged.

Research Method: qualitative user interviews with 5 respondents, chosen to understand real experiences, difficulties, and needs when using KFCKU.

Respondent criteria
- KFCKU users aged 17 to 35
- Used the app at least 2 to 3 times in the last month
- Have searched for promos in a food delivery app

Interview topics: first impressions of the layout and menu, the ordering experience, visual design (colors, text size, icons, button placement), difficulty finding promos, points, or rewards, and the one thing users would change.

Problem Statement

The main issues found were about navigation and feature arrangement
- Promos, coupons, and rewards are hard to find because their placement isn't clear or prominent enough.
- The service options on the home page (dine-in, take away, delivery) take up too much space and make the screen feel crowded.
- Adding extras to a menu item is not interactive or intuitive.
- Menu categories sit in a position that slows down finding food.
- The visual design was judged good enough, but the navigation structure and overall experience needed improvement.

Design Solutions
- Service options (Dine-In, Delivery, Take Away, Drive-Thru) crowd the home page | Move them to the checkout step, so users focus on exploring the menu first
- Coupons and promos are overlooked | Make them prominent with a promo banner, dedicated coupon and reward cards, and visual indicators
- Add-ons are confusing | Use familiar checkbox and selection components for side dishes and options
- Menu categories are hard to reach | Place categories at the top of the product list
- Checkout is cluttered | Simplify the delivery and order detail page and structure the information more clearly

Design Process

1. User research: interviews and problem analysis
2. User persona: a 19-year-old university student who loves online shopping and hunting for vouchers, but finds promos hard to locate and checkout too long
3. Customer journey map: from opening the app to payment, showing where feelings dip (home page and promo search) and the opportunity at each step
4. Empathy map: what users say, see, do, hear, and need, for students, workers, and boarding-house residents
5. Site map: limited to the features that were redesigned
6. Wireframes: home, voucher, menu, customize order, cart, and checkout
7. High-fidelity prototype: the same screens with the KFC red identity, plus a map screen to save a new delivery location
8. Usability testing: tested with 7 participants

Visual design and UI patterns
- Red as the consistent primary color, mobile layout, and generous white space
- Infinite scroll on the menu page
- Card layouts showing image, name, and price
- Search autocomplete that fills in the delivery address from the user's current location
- Sticky navigation that keeps the search bar visible while scrolling
- Z-pattern layout so users read all important information in a natural flow

Usability Testing
Seven participants completed the following tasks:
- Find and open the menu page | 7 of 7 completed with no problems
- Find and open the voucher page | 6 of 7 completed with minor difficulty
- Customize a product | 7 of 7 completed well
- Continue to checkout and choose a location and courier | 6 of 7 completed with slight confusion
- Complete the whole flow | All participants finished, with only small issues

Conclusion: the prototype was clear enough for users to complete the tasks without much confusion, so the team kept the same prototype without a further refinement round.
